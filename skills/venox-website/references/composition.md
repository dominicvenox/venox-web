# Page composition, story, and copy

Use this to turn content into a page or sequence. Build around the visitor's questions and the material available, rather than filling a standard list of sections.

## Give each section a job

Map the important content to questions such as:

- What is this, and is it relevant to me?
- What can I see or do here?
- How does it work, or why should I believe it?
- How does it compare, and what might prevent me from choosing it?
- What happens next?

Use only questions the page actually needs to answer. A portfolio may lead with the work; an unfamiliar product may need explanation before a strong offer; a documentation page should reach its answer quickly.

Write a short section sequence with one job per section. Merge sections that repeat the same claim. Move essential context earlier if later proof depends on it.

## Compose the opening as a decision

Specify the visual lead, supporting information, main action, and evidence of continuation. The opening does not have to fit every detail above the fold, but it should establish what kind of place the visitor has entered.

Useful opening relationships include a product beside its consequence, a work sample with a compact identity, or a question answered by an interactive demonstration. Choose from the content; do not apply one relationship to every website.

Media can dominate without making text unreadable. Consider the focal point, subject direction, empty image regions, contrast, and crop together. Preserve a useful first viewport when media loads slowly or cannot load.

## Create rhythm through changing roles

Vary composition when the information changes: a focused claim, an expansive image, a compact comparison, then a practical action. Rhythm comes from purposeful changes in density, scale, and attention, not alternating backgrounds mechanically.

Use recurring elements to orient the visitor: consistent action styling, familiar alignment, a repeated chapter signal, or a stable visual anchor. Repetition should make the story easier to follow.

Keep section lengths proportional to their information. A short statement stretched across several empty screens makes the user pay a navigation cost for little content.

## Make an immersive sequence legible

Plan the journey as content beats before choosing an effect. A beat records what changes, what the visitor learns, and what remains as an anchor.

| Beat | Meaning | Possible treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Establish | Show the complete object or setting | A stable image with a concise introduction |
| Explain | Reveal a relationship or detail | Retain the subject while annotations or layers change |
| Demonstrate | Show use, consequence, or transformation | A bounded visual sequence tied to the explanation |
| Resolve | Return control to the next useful action | Settle the visual and continue ordinary document flow |

This is a planning example, not a required four-part template. Every scene needs a readable resting state. If a visitor stops or reverses halfway, the surrounding content should still make sense.

Use pinned content only when retaining it helps comprehension. Use a horizontal gallery when sequence or juxtaposition matters; provide an ordinary reading path and reachable items. Avoid trapping ordinary wheel, touch, or keyboard navigation to force completion of a scene.

## Write from supplied truth

Replace vague claims with relevant detail when real detail is available. “Export a labeled PDF after each inspection” explains a capability more clearly than “Unlock seamless productivity.” Do not fabricate the export feature to improve the sentence.

Preserve actual prices, customer names, testimonials, certifications, metrics, and product limitations. Mark unknown content in planning notes; do not ship plausible fictional proof. If the page needs evidence the user has not supplied, choose an honest content structure and identify the gap.

Headings should help scanning even when read alone. Supporting copy should develop the heading rather than repeat it. Action labels should name a useful next step; their destination or result must match the promise.

Match voice to the product and audience. English copy is not a substitute for requested German copy, and literal translation may produce awkward buttons or line lengths. Inspect the real language in the design.

## Connect the full path

A call to action needs a destination and an intelligible next state. A form needs labels, appropriate inputs, validation, and honest submission behavior. A navigation item needs a route or anchor that actually exists.

Keep navigation and essential actions understandable outside the main visual sequence. Direct links, reloads, browser history, and jumping to a later section should not require watching the introduction first.

Conclude the page at its intended outcome. Repeating the hero's promise at the bottom is useful only if it helps the visitor act after the intervening evidence.
